# Outreach email templates

Nothing here has been sent. You review, personalize and send each one yourself.
Rules: one email per person, no follow-up more than once after 10-14 days, never ask for a link
in the first email's subject line, and don't send to anyone you can't tie to a specific page.

Tracker: `researcher_outreach_tracker.csv` (116 unique citations, sorted by how many of our pages cite each one).
Find each corresponding author's email on the paper itself (PubMed or the DOI link). Do not guess addresses.

---

## A. Cited researcher (heads-up + accuracy check)

Subject: Your [YEAR] [JOURNAL] study on [TOPIC], cited on a patient-facing page

Hi Dr. [LASTNAME],

I run Hypermobility Support, a free, plain-language reference site for people with hEDS and HSD. We
summarize published research for patients without overstating it, and we cite sources directly.

We cover your paper, "[TITLE]" ([JOURNAL], [YEAR]), on this page:
[PAGE URL]

I'd value one thing: if we've summarized any finding in a way you think is inaccurate or too strong,
I'd like to correct it. Everything on the page is meant to stay within what the study showed
([ONE-LINE OF THE LIMITATION WE STATE, e.g. "small sample, one clinic"]).

No reply needed if it reads fine. Thank you for the work, patients do read it.

[YOUR NAME]
Hypermobility Support, hypermobilitysupport.com

Notes: this is a courtesy plus accuracy check, not a link request. If they reply warmly, a link or
share is their call.

## Before you send anything
- The site's affiliate links exist on 9 pages. Section B is a resource pitch, so point it at pages
  without product modules where you can, to avoid looking like a sales pitch.
- Send from an address on your own domain if you have one. Many researchers ignore Gmail cold mail.
- Start with the 10-15 most-cited studies in the tracker (top rows) rather than all 116.
